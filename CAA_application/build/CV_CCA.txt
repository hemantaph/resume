Thesis research and software
Gravitational-wave lensing statistics
Lead developer of ler for lensed and unlensed GW populations and detectable rates. The project has
well-written documentation, actively maintained and updated code, and development guided by community
contributions and feedback. Work includes source and lens populations, strong-lensing selection, EPL+Shear
cross-sections, optical depths, lens-equation solutions, and detector selection. Implemented propagation of
population-hyperparameter and finite-sampling uncertainties into rates. Planned extensions cover detectable
populations and time-delay cosmography with updated models and propagated uncertainties.

SNR and detection-probability calculations
Developer of gwsnr, using partial-scaling interpolation, multiprocessing-based inner products, and artificial-
neural-network methods. Both ler and gwsnr are publicly documented and used in collaborative publications.

Eccentric waveform modelling, implementation, and data analysis
Co-developed TaylorF2Ecck, a nonspinning, low-eccentricity inspiral waveform, and implemented it in
LALSuite. Conducted Bayesian analyses of GW170817 and GW190425 and injection studies of waveform
accuracy.

Subsidiary projects and possible extensions
Line-of-sight acceleration. Collaborated on joint eccentricity and acceleration inference in GW190814, with
scope for further work using improved waveform models.
Multimessenger lensing. Co-authored lensed GW–GRB detectability forecasts. Developing Bayesian tests
for lensed GRBs using sky localisation and time delays, with possible extensions to light curves and GW–GRB
associations.

Computational and statistical methods
Programming. Python, C, C++, Fortran, Mathematica. NumPy, SciPy, Numba, JAX, and MLX. Git, Linux,
and LATEX.
Numerical methods. Monte Carlo simulation, rejection and importance sampling, inverse-transform
sampling, numerical integration, interpolation, and parallel CPU/GPU computation.
Data analysis. Bayesian parameter estimation and model comparison, nested sampling, selection effects,
population simulations, and injection studies. LALSuite and bilby.
