\section{\sc Research Projects and Contributions}

{\bf Gravitational wave strong lensing statistics:}
Lensed GWs can probe distant compact binaries, lens populations, and cosmology; interpreting them requires reliable rates and population priors that include lensing and detector selection. I lead development of \texttt{ler}, a publicly available Monte Carlo framework for simulating detectable strongly lensed and unlensed GW populations and event rates (\href{https://ler.hemantaph.com/}{documentation}; \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07526}{Phurailatpam et al., 2026}), with Otto Akseli Hannuksela, Anupreeta More, David Keitel, Chris Van Den Broeck, and other collaborators. I implemented source and lens population models, elliptical power-law lenses with external shear, semi-analytical lens-equation solvers for computing image properties, numerical cross-sections and optical depths, and detector-selection effects.

These statistics have been applied to validation of O3 lensing candidates~\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03827}{(Janquart et al., 2023)}, searches for subthreshold lensed counterparts~\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16532}{(Ng et al., 2025)}, and the rate and time delay terms entering Bayesian lensing posterior odds~\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.15463}{(Hannuksela et al., 2026)}. I also implemented propagation of uncertainties in population hyperparameters and from finite sampling into rate calculations; further analysis and publication are ongoing.

\textbf{SNR and detection probability:} Efficient SNR and detection probability calculations make large population simulations and repeated rate estimates computationally practical. I lead development of \texttt{gwsnr} with Otto Akseli Hannuksela (\href{https://gwsnr.hemantaph.com/}{documentation}; \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09888}{Phurailatpam and Hannuksela, 2024}). The Python package uses partial scaling interpolation and parallel processing of noise-weighted inner products to calculate signal-to-noise ratios, along with neural network methods to estimate detectability. I maintain the code, documentation, and user support for both packages.

{\bf Multimessenger astronomy:}
Lensed short gamma ray bursts (GRBs) can guide searches for GW counterparts below standard detection thresholds; statistical tests are needed to distinguish lensing from unrelated bursts with similar properties. With Anupreeta More, I developed forecasts for the detectability of lensed GW counterparts to lensed short GRBs and of GRB counterparts to lensed GW systems, using \texttt{ler} and \texttt{gwsnr}~\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.02536}{(More and Phurailatpam, 2025)}. With Otto Akseli Hannuksela, Albert Kong and other collaborators, I am developing Bayesian posterior odds tests of whether multiple GRBs are lensed images of the same source, initially using sky localisation and observed time delays.

{\bf Modelling gravitational waves from binaries in eccentric orbits:}
Residual orbital eccentricity probes compact binary formation channels; accurate waveforms are needed to measure it reliably and avoid biases in inferred source parameters. With Achamveedu Gopakumar, Maria Haney, Tjonnie Li, and Srishti Tiwari, I jointly developed \texttt{TaylorF2Ecck} and implemented it in a \texttt{LALSuite} fork~\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12697}{(Phurailatpam et al., 2026)}, building on our earlier work~\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07956}{(Tiwari et al., 2019)}. This analytical frequency domain inspiral approximant describes nonspinning compact binaries with low eccentricity ($e_0<0.1$, defined at $20~\mathrm{Hz}$). It extends \texttt{TaylorF2} by consistently including all leading order $e_0$ contributions from orbital dynamics, periastron advance, and GW emission, in the phase up to 3PN order and in amplitude at quadrupolar order.

I conducted Bayesian analyses of GW170817 and GW190425 and injection studies. We found no evidence for measurable eccentricity or detectable effects of periastron advance and additional harmonics; the injection studies established regimes in which these effects become distinguishable. We also found that insufficient PN phasing accuracy can bias the mass ratio posterior.

\textbf{Line of sight acceleration:} Joint inference of acceleration and eccentricity tests for a tertiary companion while accounting for their similar effects on the waveform. With Lalit Pathak and Achamveedu Gopakumar, I investigated line of sight acceleration (LOSA) induced by a massive companion and residual eccentricity in GW190814~\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21955}{(Pathak et al., 2026)}. I performed match tests and parameter estimation and contributed to interpretation. Using 32 seconds of data and \texttt{IMRPhenomXPHM} augmented with leading order LOSA and eccentricity corrections, we found a strong degeneracy between their effects on the inspiral phase, but no significant Bayesian evidence for either relative to the baseline model.

\section{\sc Future Work: Pledged and Proposed}

\textbf{LVK/GRASP pledges.} Work and effort committed to LIGO--Virgo--KAGRA projects and recorded in GRASP. My commitments include continued leadership and support of \texttt{ler} and \texttt{gwsnr}, including \texttt{ler} work weighted for ISR towards GWTC-6 and GWTC-7. Deliver updated rates and population priors for lensing analyses, including forecasts for four images and sources near caustics. For \texttt{gwsnr}, improve detection probability estimates through sensitivity calibration using injections and extend waveform support and computational performance. Eccentric waveform development and implementation are also planned GRASP activities.

\textbf{Gravitational wave lensing statistics (planned extensions).} Extend the ongoing merger histories informed by star formation with Chunglee Kim and Jiyun Park, including metallicity dependent formation efficiency and delays from formation to merger. Use the implemented uncertainty framework to assess the sensitivity of rates and lensing posterior odds to population assumptions and sampling errors. With Neha Singh, provide simulated field binary populations for rate estimates and study lensed binary black hole mergers originating from Population III stars. With Anupreeta More, extend the statistics to galaxy cluster lenses. As prospective extensions, explore millilensing and microlensing statistics with \texttt{ler}.

\textbf{Cosmography using populations (proposed study).} Test the robustness of cosmological inference from lensed event fractions and time delay distributions under alternative source and lens populations, elliptical power law lenses with external shear, and detector selection. Use simulated catalogues to assess parameter bias, credible interval coverage, and degeneracies between cosmology and population assumptions.

\textbf{Eccentric waveforms (planned collaborative development).} Shri Krishna Anand and Basundhara Ghosh, guided by Achamveedu Gopakumar, are incorporating spin effects into the eccentric waveform prescription, targeting 3PN phasing through $\mathcal{O}(e_0^6)$ and 1PN amplitudes through $\mathcal{O}(e_0^5)$. I will implement and validate these extensions using analytical checks, comparisons with independent models, and injection studies, and pursue official \texttt{LALSuite} integration. Further work will target eccentric phasing through at least 3.5PN order. Possible coordination with Chandra Kant Mishra and Kaushik Paul on these corrections is prospective. Extension through merger and ringdown is a later aim.

\textbf{Line of sight acceleration (planned extension).} Extend our GW190814 study to more events, using higher order eccentric corrections and a more general treatment of source acceleration that varies with time, such as \href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00253}{Zhao et al. (2026)}.

\textbf{Multimessenger lensing (planned candidate tests).} Complete and validate the ongoing GRB posterior odds calculation, then incorporate light curve information and explore joint associations of GWs and GRBs. Later studies of source and lens properties, time delay cosmography of individual systems, and dark matter require suitable electromagnetic observations and lens modelling. \\
